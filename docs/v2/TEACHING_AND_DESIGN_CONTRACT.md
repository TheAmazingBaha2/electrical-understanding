# Teaching and design contract

The Workbench is a long-term guided electronics tutor. A chapter becomes as many substantial lessons as its concepts require, rather than one summary or a fixed twelve lessons. The technical limit is 40 units per source. Long material must be supplied in readable excerpts; an outline cannot recover information missing from its source extraction.

Each lesson uses Overview / Learn / Interact / Quiz / Review. Learn introduces quantities, symbols, assumptions, practical meaning and intermediate worked steps in plain English. Discussion and prediction tasks ask for the learner's reasoning before showing a debrief. Each new lesson has a model-chosen number of distinct quiz checks (technical bound 1–40, with no target of ten) and 2–8 grounded widget activities, including explanation, application, changed-condition reasoning and error diagnosis. Favor multiple-choice/selectable diagnosis, true/false options and numeric checks. Written responses are optional and limited to one per quiz; they are honest self-checks, not AI-certified grades. Numeric single-quantity answers use deterministic checking. Model-selected approved slider simulators and clickable prediction tasks are the main practice. Arbitrary generated code is never executed.

Novel source content remains in its appropriate book, exam or lab path. Useful overlap belongs in Review & Reinforcement. Mathematics within a book or exam chapter stays under that source chapter. Math Practice contains explicitly requested standalone periodic refreshers, has no book requirement, and never blocks other tracks. Model inventory comparisons are advisory, not comprehensive textbook comparisons or claims of learner mastery. Every rule, activity and question cites source evidence; book/page metadata remains visible and uncertain extraction/gaps remain disclosed.

Run scan is a user-authorized sequence of saved Sol checkpoints: teaching brief, source interpretation, flexible outline, per-unit lesson drafting and separate technical/educational review. A single huge paid chapter response is intentionally avoided because output truncation and correction would endanger the whole chapter. Every paid stage retains its atomic reservation and attempt identity. Failures halt; known IDs resume freely and invalid saved JSON can be revalidated freely. Retries and revisions are separate explicit authorizations. Running is browser-orchestrated: leave/reload and explicitly Resume; no unattended scheduling is promised.

Verify data reads the original stored file and checks saved evidence, outline, draft structure, source references and review status without paid inference. It does not independently reread/interpret the chapter, prove every formula or certify pedagogical quality. Approve is a separate free human action. Only approved inventory reaches active dashboard statistics, saved quizzes and scheduled reviews. Pending drafts never do.

Visual changes use the same inventory and stable IDs. Never rewrite stored content, alter review dates, mix production/staging projects, remove financial checks or enable paused AI endpoints as a side effect of a design edit. Chapter 3's original sixteen approved units remain in Legacy; future chapters route normally. The separately authored, explicitly requested Chapter 3 curated series is additive active Associate CET Chapter 3 material, attached privately under lesson.curated_series on the original source job. Its C3-CURATED IDs are separate from the original approval. Never archive this series merely because it shares the original source scan ID; never replace original units or their approval/progress to redesign it. Preserve all three Legacy groups and their history.

Acceptance: syntax/build/module checks and unit tests pass, affected desktop/phone Chromium scenarios pass, exact-commit CI passes before staging frontend promotion, changed Edge source is deployed/read back separately, and actual data preservation is reported. No paid AI call is implied by engineering or audit authorization.

Related uploads use the same book and optional chapter/theme label. Intake identifies a complete subsection, coherent theme, or partial continuation. Each newly registered part snapshots earlier interpreted sources in that group, preserving source job, book, original printed pages and evidence IDs under namespaced references. Sol can teach supported additions and connect earlier ideas without rewriting prior approvals, inferring mastery, or inventing unseen continuation pages. With no group label, earlier book sources provide bounded context. At most eight earlier interpreted parts are included; incomplete context is disclosed. Later uploads extend the path; they do not silently enrich an already approved lesson.

## Final chapter summaries

Every newly covered chapter, from exams, books or manuals, ends with one concise `kind: summary` / `chapter_summary: true` lesson. It stays in the same source/chapter, after all ordinary lessons. Provide one Key takeaways section and a quiz prioritizing the strongest points, assumptions and common mistakes. Its length is flexible: neither sixteen questions nor one question per lesson is a quota. Several important points from one lesson can merit several checks. Existing technical bound is 1–40, not a target. Favor selectable formats; do not require essays or extra activities for the recap.

Use the shared lesson engine, current question IDs, progress aggregation and reviews. New IDs must be additive. Short summaries may use one teaching section and zero activities; the ordinary lesson depth/activity requirements stay unchanged. New completed/cohesive chapter intake requires exactly one final summary in its outline and reserves a slot within the forty-unit limit; older saved outlines remain recoverable. Partial uploads must not claim chapter completion. For chat-authored additions, mark the summary and include its covered lesson IDs/source evidence; the shared chapter index sorts summaries last. When additional parts complete a chapter, synthesize the actual covered material rather than guessing absent parts.

## Flexible workshop companions — October 13

Workshops suit source material about judgment, preparation and procedures. Each combines multiple methods selected for the material: bench arrangement, visual inspection, tool/task matching, reorderable preparation, connected cases, instrument demonstrations and searchable reference. Keep substantial plain-language explanation, assumptions, worked reasoning where relevant and debriefs. No fixed workshop duration, section length, number of workshops or quiz quota. Do not pad thin material or fragment one coherent skill just to meet a target. Existing provider response/schema bounds are operational constraints, never teaching targets; this chat-authored workshop format has no length/count validator.

Retain Overview / Learn / Interact / Quiz / Review and shared curriculum placement. Required activity checkpoints and quizzes contribute to the same chapter progress; completion needs the required activity decisions plus graded quiz criteria. Optional tool-reference browsing is not a demonstrated skill. Save activity IDs and results under additive workshopChecks using the existing owner-scoped progress/sync/conflict mechanism; do not migrate, renumber or reinterpret old answers. UI simulations are illustrative, not physical competency certifications.

Tool reference cards and workshop descriptions are independently authored; scans remain private and are not published.
